# L2: the banked scratchpad

The systolic tile's L2 (`memory/scratchpad.py`) is the first parameterization of the
[KPU tile's L2 model](https://github.com/branes-ai/embodied-ai-soc-generator/blob/main/docs/architecture/l2.md) (kpugen's page; #17 here, a port of esg#24). It replaced an idealized
scratchpad whose every bank had two read ports, which real SRAM macros don't have.

## Demand

The Streamer (`controller/execute.py`) reads two L2 rows per clock on two read ports,
and the BlockMover's LOAD writes L2 on the write port:

| Dataflow | Port A (stream) | Port B |
|---|---|---|
| OS | A tile, row-major: `R` rows, one per clock | B tile, column-major: `C` rows, in the same clocks |
| WS | the streamed tile X (Matmul: the A slot), column-major | PRELOAD's weight columns W (the B slot), beside the previous stream |
| IS | the streamed tile (Matmul: the B slot) | PRELOAD's stationary Aᵀ (the A slot) |

Each port reads consecutive rows from its tile's base, one per clock, for `max(K, R, C)`
(OS) or `max(M, R)` clocks (stationary). With `K >= R, C` (or `M >= max(R, C)`) the next
tile's reads follow with no gap. For a 64×64 int8 array that's 128 B per clock.

## Organization

- **Two bank groups, by address.** L2's rows are linear in the local address. The lower
  half (`0 .. l2_group_rows - 1`) is the **A group** and the upper half the **B group**.
  `SystolicConfig.l2_group_rows` is where the B group starts (kpugen's tile also reads it
  out as the CSR `L2_B_BASE`).
- **Banks.** Each group has `N = l2_group_banks` banks: row `r` of a group is in bank
  `r mod N` at bank row `r div N`. `N` needn't be a power of two (the decode is then a
  constant divider). The banks are 1R1W macros, pipelined, with latency
  `spad_read_delay`, and their writes are byte-masked.
- **A crossbar** gives each group's read port to the port that addresses it (port A
  first). Each bank's write port takes the BlockMover's writes in any clock.

```text
port A ──┐   ┌── A group: bank 0 … bank N-1 (rows 0 .. G-1)
         ├─×─┤
port B ──┘   └── B group: bank 0 … bank N-1 (rows G .. 2G-1)
write ─────────► the bank of the row, in either group
```

**Placement.** The ISA (`isa.command.TileBuffers`) puts A slots in the A group and B slots
in the B group. In every dataflow the two reads of a clock then land in different
groups. In IS, PRELOAD reads the A slot on port B while COMPUTE streams the B slot on port
A, still one read per group. A host that issues commands itself places its tiles the same
way, by operand, in every dataflow: **A tiles below `l2_group_rows`, B tiles at or above
it**. What matters is that the two tiles read in the same clock sit in different groups:
the A and B tiles of an OS COMPUTE, and the streamed tile and the next PRELOAD's tile in
the stationary mode. The cocotb `systolic_top` bench places its B tiles at `B_BASE`, the
B group's first row. With `l2_bank_occupancy > 1`, tile bases are also multiples of `dim` (below).

## The conflict rule

An L2 access the macros can't take sets the sticky `l2_error` (`SystolicTop.l2_error`,
`STATUS` bit 9):

- both reads of a clock in one group: port A gets its row, port B's data is wrong;
- an access to a bank still busy from its last one, when `l2_bank_occupancy > 1`;
- a row past L2.

A schedule whose tiles sit in their own groups never raises it. The unit tests check it
every clock over the GEMMs of every dataflow. It is cheap enough to keep in silicon as a
debug flag.

## Parameters and formulas

| `SystolicConfig` | Default | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `sp_capacity_kb` | 256 | L2 capacity |
| `spad_read_delay` | 4 | read latency (clocks) |
| `l2_bank_occupancy` | 1 | clocks a macro is busy per access (1 = pipelined) |
| `l2_macro_rows` | 4096 | the deepest macro, in rows (0 = no limit) |
| `l2_ideal` | False | build the idealized two-read-port reference instead |

- rows: `sp_total_rows = floor(capacity / row bytes)`, rounded down to even;
- group rows: `G = sp_total_rows / 2`;
- banks per group, with `Nd = ceil(G / l2_macro_rows)` (1 if no limit):
  - pipelined (`l2_bank_occupancy = 1`): `N = Nd`;
  - otherwise the smallest divisor of `dim` of at least `max(occupancy, Nd)`, or, if
    `Nd > dim`, the smallest multiple of `dim` of at least `Nd`. Every Streamer command
    reads `dim` consecutive rows from a base that is a multiple of `dim`, so the banks
    cycle with no break across commands, or each command reads its own aligned block,
    and a bank is never asked again within its occupancy. An occupancy above `dim` is
    refused: one buffer re-reads the same rows every `dim` clocks;
- bank rows: `ceil(G / N)`.

The bank count is set by occupancy and depth, not by latency: a pipelined macro takes a
new read every clock, so one bank per group carries the stream. (A count that doesn't
divide `dim`, such as 3 banks under a 4×4 array at occupancy 3, would reuse a bank one
clock after a command's reads wrap to the next tile: occupancy 3 gives 4 banks there.)
At 256 KiB, int8:

| dim | row (B) | L2 rows | group rows | N (occ 1) | N (occ 2) | N (occ 4) | bank rows (occ 1) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | 8 | 32768 | 16384 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4096 |
| 16 | 16 | 16384 | 8192 | 2 | 2 | 4 | 4096 |
| 24 | 24 | 10922 | 5461 | 2 | 2 | 4 | 2731 |
| 32 | 32 | 8192 | 4096 | 1 | 2 | 4 | 4096 |
| 64 | 64 | 4096 | 2048 | 1 | 2 | 4 | 2048 |

With 1024-row macros the counts are 16, 8, 6, 4 and 2. dim 24 at 1024 rows gives 6 banks
of 911 rows, neither a power of two.

## 1R1W against single-port

Built: **1R1W**. Measured against single-port (1RW) rather than built:

- **Area.** By branes-silicon's model, a dual-port (`sram_hp`, 8T) macro costs 8/6 ≈ 1.33×
  the transistors per KiB of a single-port one (`sram_hd`, 6T). kpugen's floorplan
  carries that cost.
- **Schedule.** With 1RW banks, the BlockMover's writes need clocks in which their bank
  isn't being read. In OS both groups are read on every clock of a stream. A write
  could then only take a bank the stream isn't reading in that clock. That needs
  `N >= 2` per group, and a write buffer or a write slot schedule that holds for every
  dataflow and for the corner-turned LOAD's `dim`-row bursts. 1R1W needs none of that.
  Its writes never wait.

## Measured

- **Cycles.** A double-buffered 16×16×24 Matmul in each dataflow runs in the same number
  of cycles, with the same L2 traffic, on the banked L2 and on the idealized one, and C is
  bit-identical (`test_matmul.py::test_banked_l2_sustains_the_stream_and_matches_the_ideal_model`).
- **Two reads per clock.**
  - OS reads an A and a B row in every streaming clock.
  - In the stationary mode, PRELOAD reads the B group beside the A group's stream, back to
    back, with no bubble, at occupancy 1, 2 and 3 (`test_streamer.py::TestOnTheBankedL2`).
- **Writes beside reads.** The BlockMover writes L2 in clocks where the Streamer reads it,
  with no conflict.
